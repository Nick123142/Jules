Maman, papa, j’aimerais avoir des ailes

An unvarnished exploration of psychological exploitation, domestic neglect, and the severe emotional cost of early parentification. Written with raw candor, this memoir charts the quiet terror of surviving an erratic household, the invisible burdens carried behind closed doors, and the exhausting performance of being the "easy child" to keep peace in a world prone to fracture.

“Children are brilliant, instinctual learners. They notice what brings people closer, and they notice what makes people pull away. We adjust, carving pieces of ourselves off, long before we even realize we are holding the knife.”

Author & Essayist

Jules

Writing, for me, is not about fitting into predetermined genres or adhering to polite boundaries. It is an act of reclaiming truth from silence—putting language to the experiences, observations, and raw tensions that so many spend a lifetime attempting to bury.

Whether engaging with the nuances of culture, the architectures of family dynamics, or the complex realities women carry in quiet rooms, my work is driven by an insistence on seeing things as they actually are. I write without posturing, seeking to strip away the manufactured expectations of who we are supposed to be.

“I hope readers can see themselves in these words, feel less alone in their own quiet battles, and discover that even their anger holds dignity and purpose.”
